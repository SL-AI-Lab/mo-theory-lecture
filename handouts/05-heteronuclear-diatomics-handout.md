# Handout 05: Heteronuclear Diatomics — Detailed Notes

## Key Idea: Unequal Atoms

In a heteronuclear molecule the two AO energies differ because of different
nuclear charge and electronegativity. In the LCAO secular equations, we now
have **two different Coulomb integrals**, α_A ≠ α_B.

```
| α_A − E      β − E S |
| β − E S      α_B − E |  =  0
```

The bonding MO is dominated by the atom with the **lower** AO energy (the more
electronegative atom); the antibonding MO is dominated by the higher-energy
atom.

## Polarization of MOs

- Bonding MO coefficient on the electronegative atom > coefficient on the other
- This concentrates electron density near the electronegative atom → **polar**
- Net dipole oriented toward the electronegative atom
- As the electronegativity difference grows, bonding becomes more ionic

## CO — Isoelectronic with N2

C: [He]2s²2p² (4 valence), O: [He]2s²2p⁴ (6 valence) → 10 valence electrons.

Same MO filling as N2 → **bond order 3** (one σ + two π).

- O is more electronegative → MO electron density shifted toward O
- The highest occupied MO has a lone pair character localized on **carbon**
  (explains why CO binds metals via carbon, e.g., metal carbonyls)

## NO

- N (5) + O (6) = 11 valence electrons
- Fill: σ(2s)² σ*(2s)² π(2p_x)² π(2p_y)² σ(2p_z)² π*(2p_x)¹
- Bond order = (8 − 3)/2 = 2.5
- **One unpaired electron** → paramagnetic ✓
- Fractional bond order common for open-shell diatomics

## HF

- H: 1s¹ (one valence electron)
- F: 2s²2p⁵ (seven valence electrons)
- H(1s) overlaps with F(2p_z) (same symmetry, σ)
  → σ, σ* combination
- The F 2s and the 2p_x/2p_y orbitals have no symmetry match with H 1s
  → essentially **nonbonding**
- Bond order = 1
- Electron density strongly polarized toward F → large dipole moment

## Predicting Bond Order and Multiplicity

1. Count valence electrons.
2. Build the (polarized) MO diagram.
3. Fill from lowest energy.
4. Bond order = (bonding − antibonding)/2.
5. Check for unpaired electrons → paramagnetism.

## Practice Problems

1. Compute the bond order of NO.
2. Explain why CO binds to transition metals via carbon rather than oxygen.
3. In HF, why are most of F's electrons nonbonding?
4. Which has a greater dipole, HF or HCl? (Think electronegativity difference.)

### Solutions

1. NO bond order = (8 − 3)/2 = 2.5.
2. The HOMO/lone-pair of CO is localized on carbon (MO polarized such that the
   C end carries the lone pair), so carbon is the better donor site.
3. H(1s) only overlaps with F(2p_z) by symmetry; F 2s, 2p_x, 2p_y have no
   compatible H partner → nonbonding lone pairs.
4. F is more electronegative than Cl, so the H–F bond is more polar → larger
   dipole moment (HF ≈ 1.91 D vs HCl ≈ 1.08 D).
