# Handout 06: MO Energy Ordering & s–p Mixing — Detailed Notes

## Two Orderings for Second-Row Diatomics

The relative energies of σ(2p_z) and the π(2p) pair depend on the molecule.

### Case A — significant s–p mixing (Li2 → N2)

```
σ(2s) < σ*(2s) < π(2p) < σ(2p_z)  (σ raised above π)
```
Mixing raises σ(2p_z); π(2p) unaffected. Order for filling:
σ(2s), σ*(2s), π(2p_x)=π(2p_y), σ(2p_z), ...

### Case B — little s–p mixing (O2, F2, Ne2)

```
σ(2p_z) below π(2p)
σ(2s) < σ*(2s) < σ(2p_z) < π(2p) < π*(2p) < σ*(2p_z)
```

## Origin: 2s–2p_z Symmetry

The 2s and 2p_z orbitals are both σ with respect to the internuclear axis.
When they are close in energy, the true molecular orbitals are mixtures:

```
σ_1 (bonding)  = c_1(2s) + c_2(2p_z)   (lowered)
σ_2 (antibonding) = c_1'(2s) − c_2'(2p_z)  (raised)
```

The π orbitals (2p_x, 2p_y) have no σ partner, so they are **not** affected by
2s–2p mixing.

## Why Mixing Depends on the Energy Gap

- The 2s–2p separation ΔE = E(2p) − E(2s) increases across the row
  (2s strongly bound for O, F).
- Small ΔE (B, C, N) → mixing significant → Case A
- Large ΔE (O, F, Ne) → mixing negligible → Case B

## Consequences

- The σ(2p_z) orbital is raised above the π(2p) pair in Case A.
- This changes **which MO fills first** for B2 and C2 and affects predictions.
- For **C2**, depending on treatment, the (π)⁴ (σ)⁰ vs (σ)²(π)² ordering
  changes the magnetic prediction — an instructive subtlety.

## Worked Example: Assigning the Ground State of B2

**Problem.** Using Case A ordering, write the configuration and predict
paramagnetism of B2.

**Solution.**
B2 = 8 valence (2s² 2p¹ per atom × 2). With mixing:
σ(2s)² σ*(2s)² π(2p_x)¹ π(2p_y)¹
Two unpaired π electrons → **paramagnetic** ✓ (observed triplet ground state).

## Practice Problems

1. Why does mixing affect the σ orbitals but not the π orbitals?
2. Which molecules show Case A ordering (large s–p mixing)?
3. What happens to the σ(2p_z) energy when it mixes with 2s?
4. For O2, which ordering (A or B) applies and why?

### Solutions

1. Only 2s and 2p_z share σ symmetry and can mix; 2p_x/2p_y (π) have no σ partner.
2. Li2, Be2, B2, C2, N2 (small 2s–2p gap).
3. It is raised in energy (destabilized).
4. Case B — O and F have a large 2s–2p gap, so mixing is negligible.
