# Handout 08: Magnetic Properties — Detailed Notes

## Definitions

- **Diamagnetic**: all electrons paired → net magnetic moment zero → weakly
  repelled by an applied field.
- **Paramagnetic**: one or more unpaired electrons → net magnetic moment →
  attracted by an applied field.

## Why O2 Is Paramagnetic: The Full Argument

O2 has 12 valence electrons. Using the Case B ordering:

```
σ(2s)² σ*(2s)² σ(2p_z)² π(2p_x)² π(2p_y)² π*(2p_x)¹ π*(2p_y)¹
```

Counting: 8 bonding, 4 antibonding → bond order (8−4)/2 = 2 → double bond.

The last two electrons must fill the degenerate **antibonding** π*(2p) pair.
By **Hund's rule**, they occupy **different** orbitals with **parallel** spin:

- Two unpaired electrons → S = 1
- Spin multiplicity = 2S + 1 = 3 → **triplet ^3Σ_g⁻  ground state**
- The molecule is **paramagnetic**

This is confirmed by experiment: liquid O2 is drawn into a magnetic field.

## Why Lewis/VB Theory Fails for O2

A Lewis structure O=O uses two electron pairs, all electrons paired
(diamagnetic). The VB picture likewise struggles to describe the two unpaired
electrons. MO theory is required to correctly represent this molecule.

## Hund's Rule for Molecular Orbitals

Degenerate orbitals fill one electron at a time (all parallel spin) before any
orbital is doubly occupied. Minimizing Coulomb repulsion favors the high-spin
(triplet) arrangement.

## Predicting Multiplicity

- Multiplicity M = 2S + 1
- S = |Σ m_s| = total unpaired spin
- O2: two unpaired → S = 1 → M = 3 (triplet)
- Molecules with one unpaired electron → S = ½ → M = 2 (doublet), e.g., NO

## Magnetic Classification of Common Diatomics

| Molecule | Unpaired electrons | Behavior |
|----------|-------------------|----------|
| H2       | 0                 | diamagnetic |
| N2       | 0                 | diamagnetic |
| O2       | 2                 | paramagnetic (triplet) |
| F2       | 0                 | diamagnetic |
| CO       | 0                 | diamagnetic |
| NO       | 1                 | paramagnetic (doublet) |
| B2       | 2                 | paramagnetic (triplet) |

## Practice Problems

1. Predict whether CO is paramagnetic. Justify with its MO configuration.
2. Write the MO configuration of O2 and compute its spin multiplicity.
3. Why does Hund's rule lead to paramagnetic O2?
4. Classify NO as diamagnetic or paramagnetic.

### Solutions

1. CO: all electrons paired (isoelectronic with N2 config) → diamagnetic.
2. O2: σ(2s)² σ*(2s)² σ(2p_z)² π(2p)⁴ π*(2p)² → two unpaired → multiplicity 3 (triplet).
3. Degenerate π*(2p) orbitals fill singly with parallel spins before pairing.
4. NO has one unpaired electron → paramagnetic (doublet).
