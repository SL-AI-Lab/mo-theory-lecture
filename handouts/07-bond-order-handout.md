# Handout 07: Bond Order & Bond Strength/Length — Detailed Notes

## Definition

```
Bond order = (number of bonding electrons − number of antibonding electrons) / 2
```

The bond order is the net number of two-electron bonds. It is the central
quantitative output of an MO energy-level analysis.

## Relation to Bond Length

- More bonding electrons → nuclei pulled closer together → **shorter** bond
- More antibonding population → bond lengthens, weakens
- General trend: BO 3 < BO 2 < BO 1 in bond length

| Molecule | Bond order | Bond length (pm) |
|----------|-----------|------------------|
| N2       | 3         | 109.8            |
| O2       | 2         | 120.7            |
| F2       | 1         | 141.2            |

## Relation to Bond Strength

- Higher BO → higher bond dissociation energy
- N2: ~942 kJ/mol (very strong)
- O2: ~498 kJ/mol
- F2: ~159 kJ/mol

Note that BO alone is not the whole story: F2's weak single bond also reflects
the small orbital overlap and electron–electron repulsion in a short, crowded
bond. Still, the qualitative trend holds.

## Fractional Bond Orders

Open-shell molecules can have half-integer bond orders:

- NO: bond order 2.5 (one electron in an antibonding π*)
- Reflects partial cancellation of one bonding pair by an unpaired antibonding electron

## Worked Example: Comparing Bond Orders Across a Series

**Problem.** Order N2, O2, F2 by bond order and predict relative bond lengths.

**Solution.**
- Bond orders: N2 = 3, O2 = 2, F2 = 1
- Bond lengths: F2 > O2 > N2
- Bond strengths: N2 > O2 > F2

## Practice Problems

1. Compute bond order for a molecule with 8 bonding and 4 antibonding valence electrons.
2. Why do F2's real bond strength and length deviate from a naive expectation?
3. Which is shorter: an O–O double bond or an O–O single bond?
4. Give an example of a molecule with bond order 2.5.

### Solutions

1. (8 − 4)/2 = 2.
2. F2's bond is weakened by poor orbital overlap and lone-pair (electron) repulsion,
   even though the bond order is 1; the qualitative BO trend still holds across the series.
3. The double bond is shorter.
4. NO (bond order 2.5).
