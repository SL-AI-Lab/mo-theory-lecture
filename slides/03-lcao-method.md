# Slide 03: The LCAO Method

---

## Linear Combination of Atomic Orbitals

- Approximate a molecular orbital as a sum of atomic orbitals:

  ψ_MO = c_1 ψ_1 + c_2 ψ_2 + …

- Two atomic orbitals combine to form **two** molecular orbitals:
  - **Bonding MO** (lower energy): constructive interference
  - **Antibonding MO** (higher energy): destructive interference

---

## Normalization

- Total electron density must integrate to 1
- Normalization condition: ∫ ψ* ψ dτ = 1
- Leads to the **overlap integral** S

---

## Key Integrals

- Overlap integral: `S = ∫ ψ_a ψ_b dτ`
- Coulomb integral (α): energy of an electron in a single AO
- Resonance integral (β): energy of interaction between the two AOs

- Bonding energy ≈ α + β (stabilized by |β|)
- Antibonding energy ≈ α − β (destabilized)

---

## When Do Orbitals Combine?

MO theory works best when combining orbitals that are:

1. **Similar in energy**
2. **Good spatial overlap**
3. **Same symmetry** (match)

Weak overlap and large energy gaps → little bonding (nonbonding MOs).

---

## σ and π Orbitals

- **σ bond**: electron density along internuclear axis, cylindrical symmetry
- **π bond**: electron density above/below the axis, one nodal plane
- σ orbitals form from s + s, s + p, p + p (head-on)
- π orbitals form from p + p (side-on)

---

## Antibonding Notation

- Antibonding orbitals labeled with * (star): σ*, π*
- Node between nuclei → nodal plane bisects the bond
- Occupying antibonding orbitals **reduces** net bonding

---

## Summary

- MO = linear combination of AOs
- Number of MOs = number of AOs combined
- Bonding/antibonding pair from each combination
- Overlap, energy match, symmetry govern strength
