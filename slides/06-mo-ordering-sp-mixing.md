# Slide 06: MO Energy Ordering & s–p Mixing

---

## Two Orderings Exist

For second-row diatomics there are two common MO orderings:

**Case A (Li2 → N2), s–p mixing active:**
- σ(2p_z) lies **above** π(2p) (the σ is raised by mixing)

**Case B (O2, F2, Ne2), s–p mixing small:**
- σ(2p_z) lies **below** π(2p)

---

## What Is s–p Mixing?

- The 2s and 2p_z orbitals have the same symmetry (σ)
- When close in energy, they can **mix** (hybridize at the MO level)
- Mixing **lowers** the σ(2s)-derived MO and **raises** the σ(2p_z)-derived MO
- The π orbitals are unaffected (no σ partner)

---

## When Is Mixing Significant?

- Depends on the **2s–2p energy gap** (ΔE)
- Small ΔE (B, C, N) → significant mixing (Case A)
- Large ΔE (O, F, Ne) → little mixing (Case B)

---

## Consequences of Mixing

- For B2 and C2 the ordering of σ(2p_z) and π(2p) inverts
- Changes which orbital fills first → affects bond order & multiplicity
- **C2** is predicted **paramagnetic** in some treatments (mixing matters)

---

## The MO Diagram (schematic)

```
Energy
  |          σ*(2p_z)
  |     π*(2p_x)   π*(2p_y)
  |          σ(2p_z)          <- Case B (O2, F2)
  |     π(2p_x)   π(2p_y)
  |          σ*(2s)
  |          σ(2s)
```

For Case A move σ(2p_z) above π(2p).

---

## Why It Matters

- Correctly predicts the electron configuration of each molecule
- Explains paramagnetism and bond order
- Essential for accurate spectroscopy prediction

---

## Summary

- s–p mixing depends on the 2s–2p energy gap
- Small gap (Li2–N2): mixing inverts σ/p ordering
- Large gap (O2–F2): no significant mixing
- π orbitals are never involved in s–p mixing
