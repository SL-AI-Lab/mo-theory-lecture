# Slide 04: Homonuclear Diatomics (H2, He2, N2, O2, F2)

---

## Building the MO Diagram for H2

- Two H atoms: each contributes a 1s orbital
- Combination gives σ(1s) and σ*(1s)
- Two electrons fill σ(1s) (spin-paired)
- Bond order = 1 → H2 is stable

---

## He2 — Would It Bond?

- Each He contributes 1s; MOs: σ(1s), σ*(1s)
- Four electrons fill σ and σ*
- Bonding = antibonding → **bond order = 0**
- He2 is **not** stable as a diatomic molecule (predicted, and not observed)

---

## MO Ordering for B2–F2 (no s–p mixing)

For Li2 → F2 (roughly, ignoring s–p mixing):

- σ(1s), σ*(1s) (core, doubly occupied)
- σ(2s), σ*(2s)
- σ(2p_z)
- π(2p_x) = π(2p_y) (degenerate)
- π*(2p_x) = π*(2p_y)
- σ*(2p_z)

---

## N2

- 10 valence electrons
- Configuration:
  σ(2s)² σ*(2s)² π(2p_x)² π(2p_y)² σ(2p_z)²
- Bond order = (8 − 2)/2 = **3**
- Triple bond: one σ + two π
- Diamagnetic (all electrons paired) ✓ observed

---

## O2

- 12 valence electrons
- Configuration (no s–p mixing assumed here for illustration):
  σ(2s)² σ*(2s)² σ(2p_z)² π(2p)⁴ π*(2p)²
- **Hund's rule**: the two π* electrons occupy **different** orbitals, same spin
- Bond order = (8 − 4)/2 = **2** (double bond)
- **Paramagnetic** (unpaired electrons) ✓ explains observed magnetism

---

## F2

- 14 valence electrons
- σ(2s)² σ*(2s)² σ(2p_z)² π(2p)⁴ π*(2p)⁴
- Bond order = (8 − 6)/2 = **1** (single bond) ✓ matches experiment

---

## Bond Order Formula

```
Bond order = (number of bonding electrons − number of antibonding electrons) / 2
```

- **H2**: (2−0)/2 = 1
- **N2**: (8−2)/2 = 3
- **O2**: (8−4)/2 = 2
- **F2**: (8−6)/2 = 1

---

## Trend: Bond Order, Length, Strength

- Higher bond order → **shorter** and **stronger** bond
- N2 (BO 3): very short, very strong
- O2 (BO 2), F2 (BO 1): progressively weaker and longer
- Matches experimental bond energies/lengths

---

## Summary

- Electron configurations determine bond order
- He2 unpredicted/unobserved (BO 0)
- N2 triple bond, diamagnetic
- O2 double bond, paramagnetic (Hund's rule)
- F2 single bond, diamagnetic
