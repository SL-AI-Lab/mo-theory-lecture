# Slide 02: Atomic Orbitals Review

---

## Quantum Numbers

Four quantum numbers describe each electron in an atom:

| n | l | m_l | m_s |
|---|-----|------|------|
| principal | azimuthal | magnetic | spin |
| shell | subshell | orbital | electron |
| n = 1,2,3,… | l = 0…(n−1) | −l…+l | ±½ |

---

## Orbital Types (l)

- **s** (l = 0): spherical, n − 1 radial nodes, no angular nodes
- **p** (l = 1): dumbbell, 3 orientations (p_x, p_y, p_z), one angular node
- **d** (l = 2): cloverleaf, 5 orientations (d_xy, d_xz, d_yz, d_x2−y2, d_z2)
- **f** (l = 3): complex shapes, 7 orientations

Number of orbitals per subshell = 2l + 1.

---

## Radial and Angular Nodes

- Nodes are regions where the wavefunction = 0
- Total nodes = n − 1
- Radial nodes = n − l − 1
- Angular nodes = l
- More nodes → higher energy

---

## Orbital Energy Ordering (Multielectron)

- For H (one electron): energy depends only on n
- For multielectron atoms: penetration and shielding
- Aufbau order: 1s 2s 2p 3s 3p 4s 3d 4p …

---

## Why Does Shape Matter for Bonding?

- **Overlap** of orbitals determines bond strength
- Only orbitals with matching symmetry and similar energy combine strongly
- s orbitals are compact and overlap head-on (σ bonds)
- p orbitals overlap head-on (σ) or side-on (π)

---

## Recap — Nodes & Shapes

- Each orbital: rationalize shape from (n, l, m_l)
- Nodes increase energy
- Shapes → symmetry → which orbitals can combine
