# Slide 05: Heteronuclear Diatomics (CO, NO, HF)

---

## Effect of Electronegativity

- In heteronuclear molecules the two atoms are **not equivalent**
- The more electronegative atom contributes **more** to bonding MOs
- Its AOs are **lower in energy**
- Molecular orbitals are **polarized** toward the electronegative atom

---

## General Principle: Energy Match

- Bonding MO mostly localized on the **more electronegative** atom
- Antibonding MO mostly localized on the **less electronegative** atom
- Larger electronegativity difference → more unequal sharing, ionic character
- Smaller difference → more covalent, symmetric MOs

---

## CO (isoelectronic with N2)

- 10 valence electrons (C: 4, O: 6)
- Same MO filling as N2 → **bond order 3** (triple bond)
- O is more electronegative → MOs polarized toward O
- Nonbonding lone pair localized on C (explains C as the Lewis-base site)

---

## NO

- 11 valence electrons
- One electron in an antibonding π* orbital (unpaired)
- Bond order = (8 − 3)/2 = **2.5**
- **Paramagnetic** (unpaired electron)
- Half-order bonds are observed for radicals

---

## HF

- H: 1s (one electron); F: 2s, 2p (seven electrons)
- H(1s) overlaps mainly with F(2p_z) → σ and σ*
- Remaining F p and s electrons are essentially **nonbonding**
- Bond order = 1 (single bond)
- Electron density shifted toward F (polar bond)

---

## Predicting Polarity

- In MO terms polarity arises from **unequal AO contributions**
- Net dipole moment depends on the asymmetry of electron distribution
- HF has a strong dipole (very electronegative F)

---

## Summary

- Heteronuclear MOs = polarized versions of homonuclear diagrams
- Electronegativity lowers AO energy and shifts electron density
- CO: triple bond, C lone pair at the carbon
- NO: BO 2.5, paramagnetic
- HF: single bond, polarized toward F
