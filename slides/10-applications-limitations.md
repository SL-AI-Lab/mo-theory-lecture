# Slide 10: Applications & Limitations

---

## Applications of MO Theory

- Predicting **magnetic properties** (paramagnetic O2, NO)
- Rationalizing **bond order**, bond length, bond strength
- Understanding **spectroscopy** (electronic transitions, ionization)
- Describing **conjugation** and aromaticity in organic molecules
- **Computational chemistry**: basis for DFT, ab initio methods
- **Solid-state physics**: band theory, conductors/semiconductors
- Predicting reactivity via frontier orbitals (HOMO–LUMO)

---

## Frontier Orbital Concepts

- **HOMO**: highest occupied molecular orbital (donor)
- **LUMO**: lowest unoccupied molecular orbital (acceptor)
- HOMO–LUMO gap → chemical stability & color
- Basis of Fukui's frontier molecular orbital theory

---

## Limitations of Simple MO Theory

- **Hartree–Fock/simple LCAO neglect electron correlation**
- Correct relative energies often require post-HF or DFT
- Neglect of **electron correlation** → systematic errors in bond dissociation
- Large molecules → computational cost
- Qualitative MO diagrams can be ambiguous (s–p mixing ordering)

---

## When Simple MO Fails

- Predicting **dissociation energies** quantitatively (needs correlation)
- Certain excited-state orderings
- Open-shell systems need care (spin, correlation)
- Extended systems require periodic treatment

---

## Beyond Simple MO

- **Density Functional Theory (DFT)**: includes correlation via functional
- **Coupled Cluster (CCSD(T))**: high accuracy for ground states
- **Configuration Interaction (CI)**: excited states
- **Multireference methods**: for strongly correlated/bond-breaking cases

---

## Take-Home

- MO theory is a powerful **qualitative** and conceptual framework
- It is the foundation of modern computational chemistry
- For quantitative accuracy, pair with electron-correlation methods
